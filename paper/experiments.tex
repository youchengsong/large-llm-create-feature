\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate KnowFeat on seven public tabular benchmarks. Our experiments aim to answer three research questions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1}: Does KnowFeat improve predictive performance compared to existing AutoFE and LLM-based methods?
    \item \textbf{RQ2}: What is the contribution of domain knowledge? (Ablation study)
    \item \textbf{RQ3}: How does KnowFeat balance feature quantity vs.\ quality?
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{subsec:setup}

All datasets are standard tabular benchmarks from OpenML, covering diverse domains including finance, healthcare, marketing, and census data. Dataset sizes range from 683 to 48,842 samples, with feature counts from 4 to 20, providing a representative evaluation setting for automated feature engineering.

\subsubsection{Baselines}
We compare KnowFeat against five baselines:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{No-FE}: XGBoost trained on original features without any feature engineering.
    \item \textbf{OpenFE}~\cite{zhang2024openfe}: State-of-the-art transformation-based AutoFE that searches over operator-operand combinations.
    \item \textbf{AutoFeat}~\cite{horn2020set}: Polynomial and interaction feature generation with L1 selection.
    \item \textbf{CAAFE}~\cite{hollmann2024llmsgeneratefinanciallyrelevantfeatures}: LLM-based iterative feature generation with logistic regression feedback.
    \item \textbf{FeatLLM}~\cite{han2024featllm}: LLM-based rule extraction and binary feature generation.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Evaluation Protocol}
All methods are evaluated using XGBoost (n\_estimators=200, max\_depth=6, learning\_rate=0.1) with stratified 80/20 train-test splits across 5 random seeds. The primary metric is AUC. We report mean and standard deviation across seeds.

\subsection{RQ1: Performance Comparison}
\label{subsec:rq1}

OpenFE achieves the best results on 3 datasets (diabetes, heart, bank\_marketing), which is expected given that it performs exhaustive operator-operand search with LightGBM-based selection---a purely data-driven approach that is highly effective when sufficient transformation combinations exist. However, OpenFE generates 34--50 features per dataset, while KnowFeat achieves competitive or superior performance with 12--46 features (see Section~\ref{subsec:rq3}).

CAAFE and FeatLLM perform close to or below the No-FE baseline on most datasets, suggesting that their generated features often introduce noise rather than signal. This is consistent with their lack of rigorous verification: without L2 and L3 filtering, low-quality features dilute the model's predictive power.

\textbf{Average rank analysis.} Across all seven datasets, KnowFeat achieves the best average rank (1.4), followed by OpenFE (1.7). AutoFeat and CAAFE rank near the bottom (4.8 and 4.9), indicating inconsistent performance. FeatLLM (4.3) generates the most features yet rarely improves over the No-FE baseline, confirming that quantity without verification does not translate to quality.

\subsection{RQ2: Ablation Study}
\label{subsec:rq2}

To quantify the contribution of domain knowledge, we compare the \textit{schema\_only} variant (Level 0: no domain knowledge, only dataset metadata) against the \textit{full} variant (all knowledge levels). Results are shown in Table~\ref{tab:ablation}.

The results reveal several important findings:

\textbf{Knowledge drives feature generation.} On the diabetes dataset, the schema-only variant generates \emph{zero} features that pass the L3 verification---the LLM cannot create useful features from column names alone without domain understanding. With full knowledge, KnowFeat generates 24 accepted features and improves AUC by +0.016 over No-FE.

\textbf{Knowledge improves feature quality.} On five of the six remaining datasets, the full variant outperforms schema\_only, with gains ranging from +0.001 (adult) to +0.012 (blood\_transfusion). On blood\_transfusion, both variants generate 12 features, indicating that the improvement comes from feature \emph{quality}, not quantity---domain knowledge guides the agent toward more predictive transformations. The largest gain (+0.012) occurs on blood\_transfusion, where clinical knowledge about recency and frequency patterns provides signal that schema metadata alone cannot capture.

\textbf{breast\_w exception.} On breast\_w, schema\_only slightly outperforms full ($-$0.001 AUC). We attribute this to the dataset's near-ceiling baseline (AUC $>$ 0.99): with so little room for improvement, additional knowledge-guided features may introduce marginal noise rather than signal.

\subsection{Provenance and Interpretability}
\label{subsec:provenance}

A distinctive advantage of KnowFeat is that every generated feature comes with a provenance card that records: (1) the knowledge assets that inspired the feature, referenced by ID; (2) a structured reasoning chain explaining \emph{why} the feature is relevant; and (3) the computation steps from raw fields to the final value. This level of traceability is essential for deployment in regulated industries where model features must be auditable and explainable. We illustrate this capability through detailed case studies in Section~\ref{sec:case_study}.
