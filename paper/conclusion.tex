\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented KnowFeat, a knowledge-guided feature engineering framework that combines hierarchical domain knowledge injection, multi-stage verification (L1--L3), and intelligent search strategies within an LLM agent. Our experiments demonstrate that KnowFeat generates fewer but higher-quality features compared to existing methods, achieving competitive or superior performance on six public datasets and a real-world AML dataset with 200K transactions. The ablation study confirms that domain knowledge is essential for generating effective features, particularly on datasets where schema-only metadata is insufficient. Every generated feature is traceable to specific knowledge assets, providing the auditability required in regulated domains.

\textbf{Limitations and Future Work.}
Several directions remain for future investigation.
First, the SimECNY dataset's strong 93-feature baseline leaves limited room for improvement, compressing inter-method differences. We plan to evaluate on reduced-feature settings (e.g., randomly sampling 40 of 93 baseline features) to more clearly demonstrate knowledge contributions.
Second, our current knowledge hierarchy is manually organized; automating knowledge extraction and structuring from heterogeneous sources (regulations, case law, expert interviews) is a promising direction.
Third, KnowFeat currently uses a single LLM for all reasoning; a multi-agent architecture with specialized agents for knowledge retrieval, feature proposal, and verification could further improve efficiency and quality.
Finally, extending KnowFeat to other high-stakes domains such as healthcare, cybersecurity, and insurance fraud detection would validate the generality of the knowledge-guided approach.
