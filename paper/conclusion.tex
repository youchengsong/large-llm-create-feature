\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented KnowFeat, a knowledge-guided feature engineering framework that combines structured domain knowledge injection, multi-stage verification (L1--L3), and explainable provenance within an LLM agent. Our central finding is that \emph{verification quality matters more than generation quantity}: a small set of verified features consistently outperforms much larger sets of unverified features, confirming that the three-stage pipeline is the key differentiator. Structured provenance records enable efficient compliance review by tracing every feature to its knowledge assets and reasoning chain. On eight public datasets and an AML benchmark, KnowFeat achieves competitive performance while producing fewer, higher-quality features with full traceability.

\textbf{Limitations and Future Work.}
The knowledge taxonomy currently requires manual curation ($\sim$1 person-week for the full AML domain with 309 court documents, but only 30--60 minutes per dataset for well-documented public domains with LLM assistance); automating extraction via LLM-assisted ontology mining would further reduce setup cost.
L3 retrains from scratch per candidate; incremental or amortized evaluation would lower overhead. Extending KnowFeat to other high-stakes domains (e.g., healthcare, cybersecurity), evaluating alternative backbone LLMs, and integrating real-world AML datasets are promising directions.
