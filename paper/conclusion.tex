\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented KnowFeat, a knowledge-guided feature engineering framework that combines structured domain knowledge injection, multi-stage verification (L1--L3), and explainable provenance within an LLM agent. Across eight public datasets, KnowFeat achieves the best descriptive average rank, although paired tests do not establish statistical superiority. Cross-model checks show mostly positive but small, non-universal gains over No-FE for XGBoost, LightGBM, and CatBoost. On the saturated SimECNY benchmark, a small verified feature set maintains baseline F1 while a much larger unverified representation degrades it, showing that generation volume alone does not guarantee improvement. Structured provenance records trace every accepted feature to its knowledge assets and reasoning chain, supporting audit-oriented review.

\textbf{Limitations and Future Work.}
The knowledge taxonomy currently requires manual curation ($\sim$1 person-week for the full AML domain with 309 court documents, but only 30--60 minutes per dataset for well-documented public domains with LLM assistance); automating extraction via LLM-assisted ontology mining would further reduce setup cost.
L3 retrains from scratch per candidate; incremental or amortized evaluation would lower overhead. Three independent end-to-end runs on heart show stable frozen-test AUC (std\,$\leq$\,0.002) despite very low feature-name overlap (Jaccard\,$\approx$\,0.04), confirming that the verification pipeline---not token-level LLM determinism---drives consistent performance. Extending KnowFeat to other high-stakes domains (e.g., healthcare, cybersecurity), evaluating alternative backbone LLMs, and integrating real-world AML datasets are promising directions.
