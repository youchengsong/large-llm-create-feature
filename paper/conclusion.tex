\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented KnowFeat, a knowledge-guided feature engineering framework that combines hierarchical domain knowledge injection, multi-stage verification (L1--L3), and auditable provenance within an LLM agent. On seven public datasets, KnowFeat achieves the best AUC on 4 of 7 and the best average rank (1.4) among seven methods, while requiring significantly fewer features than alternative LLM-based methods. On a real-world AML dataset with 200K transactions, 18 knowledge-guided features improve Recall by +2.1pp and F1 by +1.5pp over a strong 93-feature baseline---gains that are robust across different class balancing settings. The ablation study confirms that domain knowledge is essential, and every generated feature is traceable to specific knowledge assets.

These results have two broader implications. First, the knowledge hierarchy demonstrates that structured external knowledge can systematically guide LLMs beyond their parametric knowledge, producing features that domain experts recognize as meaningful rather than artifacts of data-driven search. Second, the provenance cards generated by KnowFeat provide a practical pathway toward regulatory compliance for ML-based decision systems: each feature can be audited, challenged, and justified using the same evidence trail that compliance officers already use in manual processes. This framework generalizes beyond AML to any regulated domain where feature interpretability and traceability are prerequisites for deployment.

\textbf{Limitations and Future Work.}
Our current knowledge hierarchy is manually organized by domain experts; automating knowledge extraction and structuring from heterogeneous sources (regulations, case law, expert interviews) would reduce the setup cost for new domains. KnowFeat currently uses a single LLM for all reasoning; a multi-agent architecture with specialized agents for knowledge retrieval, feature proposal, and verification could improve efficiency. Extending KnowFeat to additional high-stakes domains such as cybersecurity and insurance fraud detection would further validate the generality of the approach and the knowledge-guided paradigm.
