\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented KnowFeat, a knowledge-guided feature engineering framework that combines structured domain knowledge injection, multi-stage verification (L1--L3), and explainable provenance within an LLM agent. On seven public datasets, KnowFeat achieves the best average rank (1.4) among eight methods. On a realistic AML dataset (synthesized to match production digital currency transaction patterns) with 200K transactions, 18 verified features improve Recall by 2.1\% and F1 by 1.5\% over a strong 93-feature baseline, with gains robust across different class balancing settings and transferable to alternative downstream models (LightGBM, CatBoost).

Three broader implications emerge. First, structured external knowledge guides LLMs beyond parametric knowledge, producing features that domain experts recognize as meaningful. Second, provenance cards provide a practical pathway toward regulatory compliance for ML-based decision systems. Third, the contrast between KnowFeat's 18 verified features and FeatLLM's 1{,}031 unverified features highlights that verification quality matters more than generation quantity.

\textbf{Limitations and Future Work.}
The knowledge taxonomy requires manual curation ($\sim$3 person-weeks for AML); automating extraction would reduce setup cost. L3 retrains from scratch per candidate; incremental updates would reduce overhead. Our experiments use a single backbone LLM (DeepSeek-V4-0324-Flash); evaluating how different LLMs affect generation quality and verification pass rates remains future work. Extending to additional high-stakes domains (e.g., healthcare, cybersecurity) and integrating RAG for dynamic knowledge retrieval are promising directions.
